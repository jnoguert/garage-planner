Drag to paint with the selected tool; the Eraser removes anything (wall, bay, entrance, exit or car) under the cursor. "Entrance and exit" paints a single space that acts as both — use it if your real floor plan has one door used in both directions, rather than a separate entrance and exit. With the Straight line tool: drag from where you want the fixed end — it stays horizontal or vertical depending on which way you drag further. Double-click a line's metre label to change its length while keeping the start end fixed. With the Car tool: choose the angle first (slider or R/E keys) — the ghost shows it — and click to plant the car already oriented. To re-orient one already placed, click it again and drag. Del removes the selected one.

    What it checks

    Always the worst case: every car parked in its own bay. No car is assumed to have already left or arrived — each one has to be able to move with the others exactly as they are now.

    Result

      What it checks and what it does not
      • Each car is modelled as a rectangle steered at the front wheels (kinematic bicycle model). The exit trajectory is searched for with forward and reverse manoeuvres; if no valid one exists, the car is flagged.
      • Each car is checked independently, with all the others parked exactly where they are now: it is never assumed that some other car has already left to make room. If a car could only get out after another one was moved first, that car is flagged as having no exit (blocked), even though the other one can indeed get out. Simultaneous traffic, queues, crossings and right of way are not checked.
      • Collision is exact: rectangle against rectangle for cars and rectangle against segment for the walls of an imported floor plan. Walls you draw by hand, on the other hand, are 10 cm cells and get rounded to the grid.
      • The front and rear overhangs of each model are an estimate: manufacturers publish the length and the wheelbase, but rarely the split. It slightly affects the turn.
      • A car counts as out when the centre of its body reaches an exit cell, not when it has fully left the site.
      • Everything is flat and 2D: no ramps, slopes, headroom, kerbs or level changes. Pillars only exist if you draw them.
      • Space to open doors, accessibility, pedestrian routes and building regulations are not checked. The turning diameter is kerb to kerb; manufacturers sometimes publish the wall-to-wall figure, which is larger.
      • "No exit" can mean different things, and the simulator tells them apart: blocked by the other cars as they are parked now (it would fit if they were moved), or no room to manoeuvre even alone on the site.
